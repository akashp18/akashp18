## Operations Hub

### Enterprise Manufacturing Operations Platform

One of the projects I'm most proud of is **Operations Hub**, an internal manufacturing operations platform I built to solve a problem I kept seeing in the plant.

Important production information existed across different systems, databases, machines, paperwork and operational processes. People could get the information, but understanding **what was happening right now** often required pulling information together manually.

The goal wasn't to build another dashboard.

It was to create a reliable operational layer between the plant floor and the people making decisions.

Operations Hub brings production information together so operations, production and management can see the current state of the plant, identify exceptions and investigate issues while there is still time to respond.

The engineering challenge was making this work with an existing manufacturing environment rather than replacing it.

I worked with existing Oracle data, production systems and operational workflows and built the services needed to turn that information into something useful at the point of decision.

### Impact

The biggest value is **visibility and time-to-detection**.

Instead of discovering an issue later through paperwork, a report or a conversation, stakeholders can identify operational exceptions as they develop and investigate them while there is still an opportunity to respond.

That changes the role of software from simply recording what happened to helping people understand:

**What is happening right now?**

Operations Hub also brought together many parts of my engineering experience in one system: application development, backend engineering, databases, infrastructure, industrial systems and manufacturing operations.

It has also become a foundation for the next problem I'm working on:

**How can AI safely interact with operational data and tools to help people investigate problems faster?**

## A Few Things I've Learned Along the Way

A clean API doesn't help if the machine on the other side doesn't have one.

The database is often more important than the UI.

Factory software has to survive real people, real machines and real downtime.

Automation is useful only when you can understand what happened when it fails.

AI agents need boundaries just as much as they need intelligence.

And after moving between mobile, backend, industrial systems and infrastructure, I've found that the most interesting engineering problems usually live **between systems**.
